# Design context

## Users

A single operator — the person who owns the hardware — running game servers for themselves.
No customers, no support burden, no onboarding to design for. The reader of every screen already
knows what a node, an egg and an allocation are.

Two real contexts of use:

- **At a desk**, most of the time: reading console output, editing config files, watching resource
  graphs while something compiles or a world generates. Long sessions, dense information, keyboard.
- **On a phone**, occasionally but urgently: a server has crashed or is misbehaving and needs to be
  restarted, or a log needs checking, away from the desk. Short sessions, one task, thumbs.

The phone case is rare but it is the one where the interface failing is most costly.

## Brand personality

**Instrumented, unfussy, quietly precise.**

This is an operator's instrument, not a product with a marketing page. It should feel like the front
panel of something well built: everything legible at a glance, nothing decorative competing with the
data. It never explains itself twice and never celebrates a routine action.

## Aesthetic direction

Dark first, and dark is what the panel is designed against — the context is terminal-adjacent and
usually after dark. A light theme is supported and follows the system preference by default, so
every surface has to hold up in both. That includes the terminal and the file editor, which both
read their colours from the theme tokens rather than pinning a palette of their own.

The default theme carries the identity below. Five ported themes sit alongside it — Tokyo Night,
Catppuccin, Nord, Gruvbox, Rosé Pine — because an operator who has already picked a palette for
their terminal and editor will want the panel between them to match. They are ports, not new
designs: the published palettes, darkened where a light variant would otherwise put a state colour
below 4.5:1 on its background. Judge a change against the default theme, then check it in one
ported light theme, where surfaces are closest together.

Restrained identity built on the existing shadcn/Base UI structure rather than a visual rewrite:

- Neutrals tinted toward a single cool hue so surfaces feel deliberate rather than default grey.
- Colour is scarce and carries meaning. Green means running, red means failed or destructive, amber
  means transitional or locked. Chart series are the only other place colour appears.
- Typography stays a fixed rem scale (Geist Variable). Hierarchy comes from weight and spacing, not
  from more sizes.

Anti-references: the generic dark admin template with cyan glow and gradient headings; anything that
adds a decorative element where a number would do.

## Design principles

1. **Density is a feature.** This user wants more on screen, not more whitespace. Do not pad for
   elegance at the cost of a visible row.
2. **Adapt for the phone, never amputate.** Every action available at a desk stays available on a
   phone. Tables become readable stacks; they do not lose their columns.
3. **Colour is signal.** If a colour does not encode state or distinguish a data series, it should
   not be there.
4. **The console is the product.** When space is contested, the terminal wins.
5. **Say it once.** No heading that repeats the page title, no helper text that restates the label.
